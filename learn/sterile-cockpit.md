# Sterile Cockpit 🤐

## What it is

During critical phases, nothing is said or done that is not required for the task. No
side conversations, no unrelated questions, no "while I have you".

It is a rule about *when*, not about rudeness. The same chat that builds a team at cruise
altitude kills people on approach.

## Origin & evidence

A US federal regulation since 1981, adopted after the NTSB repeatedly found non-essential
conversation in the causal chain of approach and landing accidents. The case usually cited
is Eastern Air Lines 212, which flew into terrain short of Charlotte in 1974 while the
crew discussed politics; the NTSB found a lack of altitude awareness caused by distraction
from idle chatter. Air Florida 90 in 1982 was found to involve a failure to keep the
cockpit sterile during preflight checks.

The regulation itself is unusually direct:

> "No flight crewmember may engage in, nor may any pilot in command permit, any activity
> during a critical phase of flight which could distract any flight crewmember from the
> performance of his or her duties."
>
> "Critical phases of flight includes all ground operations involving taxi, takeoff and
> landing, and all other flight operations conducted below 10,000 feet, except cruise
> flight."
>
> 14 CFR § 121.542, Flight Crewmember Duties.
> https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/14/121.542

Two details transfer well. First, the rule names *specific banned activities* — eating,
non-essential conversation, reading unrelated material — rather than asking for good
judgement. Second, it puts the burden on the person in charge to **permit** nothing, not
merely to abstain personally.

## How to use it

- **Declare it, aloud and by name.** "Sterile until the migration completes." People
  cannot observe a rule nobody invoked.
- **Define the window before you start**, as part of the brief, so it is not a judgement
  call made while busy.
- **Say what is still allowed.** Anything about the change, and anything safety-related,
  always.
- **The person in charge enforces it.** "Hold that until we're done" is the whole
  intervention.
- **Declare the end too**, or it decays and nobody believes the next one.

## Worked example

Mid-migration, someone posts a question in the incident channel about next week's release
schedule. The operator answers it, loses their place in the runbook, and re-runs a step.
Nothing about the question was unreasonable — it simply arrived inside a window that had
never been declared closed.

## Limits

It suppresses the chatter that also carries situational awareness, so a window kept sterile
too long starves the team of context. Keep it tight around genuinely critical steps, and
never let it suppress a safety concern: those are always in scope, which is precisely what
[PACE](./pace-and-two-challenge.md) is for.
